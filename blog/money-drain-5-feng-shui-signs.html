Always Short on Money? 5 Feng Shui 'Wealth Drains' Hiding in Your Home

Cluttered Entry: The First Thing Qi Sees

Your entryway is where all energy enters your home. If it's stuffed with shoes, packages, and random clutter, the qi hits a wall before it can flow inward. From an environmental psychology standpoint, coming home to chaos every day reinforces a subconscious pattern of "disorder." This directly impacts financial decisions — impulse buys, overlooked bills, bad timing on investments. Keep the entry clear. Your money flow starts here.

Stove Facing the Door: Your Kitchen Wealth Is Leaking

In spatial energy theory, the stove represents your wealth reserve. When the stove faces a door or bathroom entrance directly, the stability of your financial energy is compromised. The practical result? Money comes in, but it leaves just as fast through unexpected expenses. The fix is simple: a half-screen or hanging beads between the stove and the door.

Bathroom in the Center: A Slow Poison for Your Finances

The bathroom is where energy goes to be discharged. If it sits in the exact center of your home — the 'Tai Chi point' — its draining energy radiates outward to every room. You'll find yourself working harder than ever for diminishing returns. Check your floor plan. If the center is a bathroom, this alone explains the financial friction.

Through-Draft: Money Comes In and Immediately Leaves

When your front door aligns perfectly with a back door or large window, qi flows straight through without settling. In practical terms: decent income, empty wallet at month-end. The simplest solution is a wind chime between the two openings, or heavy curtains that slow the energy down.

Beam Over Bed: The Subconscious Poverty Signal

A beam or ceiling fan directly above your bed creates a pressure zone in your sleep space. Sleeping under a compression point reprograms your subconscious toward caution and scarcity. You'll notice it in meetings: you hesitate to name your price, you undersell yourself, you let opportunities pass. Not mysticism — overhead visual pressure really does suppress decision confidence.
