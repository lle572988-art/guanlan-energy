**ADJUSTMENT #1: THE COMMAND POSITION**

*The most overlooked principle in modern homes.*

The Command Position is simple: the main seat in any room should face the entrance without being directly in line with the door. In your living room, your bed, your desk — the position from which you spend the most time.

**Why it works:** When you can see who or what enters without being startled, your nervous system relaxes. This isn't superstition — it's spatial psychology. Your body knows when it's vulnerable.

**Try it now:** Sit in your main chair. Can you see the door? If not, rearrange so you can. If that's impossible (your desk faces a wall), place a small mirror so you can see the reflection of the entrance. 30 seconds. Instant shift.

---

**ADJUSTMENT #2: THE CLUTTER CEILING**

*The rule that changed how I see every room.*

Draw an imaginary horizontal line at eye level. Everything below this line is "ground energy." Everything above is "mind energy."

**The fix:** Clear all surfaces below eye level — coffee tables, nightstands, kitchen counters. Not storage (cupboards are fine), but *visible surfaces*.

**Why it works:** Below-eye-level clutter creates subconscious heaviness. It tells your brain "there's unfinished business here." When you clear surfaces, your mind releases the load — even if you didn't know you were carrying it.

**Try it now:** Pick one surface in the room you're in. Remove everything except one object. Breathe. Feel the difference.

**ADJUSTMENT #3: THE ELEMENT BALANCE SWAP**

*The fastest way to change a room's feeling.*

Every room has a dominant element. Most rooms have too much of one thing. The fix is a single swap.

| If your room feels... | Its dominant element is... | Swap in this element... | By adding one of these... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chaotic, restless | Wood | Metal | A grey cushion, a metal frame |
| Hot, tense, anxious | Fire | Water | Something blue, a small bowl of water |
| Heavy, stuck, stagnant | Earth | Wood | A small plant, a green textile |
| Cold, sterile, distant | Metal | Fire | A warm lamp, a red/orange object |
| Drained, damp, heavy | Water | Earth | A ceramic object, terracotta tones |

**Try it now:** Look around the room. Which of the five feeling words fits best? Add one object from the "swap" column. That's it.
